### Findings
1. **The stalled seeds barely pause.** s8 lost 7.4 hold minutes to defenses-down pauses; s9 lost none, because **s9 never started a victory hold at all**. Several won seeds paused longer (s1 43.5, s6 22.6). So hold pauses are not what keeps s8 and s9 open, and H1 is wrong.
2. **The stalled seeds are where the fighting is.** s8 and s9 hold 70 of the 87 defenses-down events and most of the down-minutes, matching their 67 and 74 Offensives (J0) and their high engagement (A2 about 200 s, idle 5–6%). The coalition's nodes keep getting knocked down and fought over, so it rarely or never holds all victory nodes at once long enough to start a hold.
3. **Most downs are enemy fire, not supply.** 75% of downs pooled (65 of 87) and 79% in the stalled seeds (55 of 70) are fire. Starvation is a real minority (about a quarter), not the driver.
4. **What stalls is reaching a hold, not finishing one.** The stalled seeds look like a contested front that neither side breaks: the Static retakes or knocks down coalition nodes often enough that the hold condition is rarely met. Why it happens in these two seeds and not the others is [UNVERIFIED].

## Decisions (lead; owner may veto)
- **Don't build O1** (a defense-repair truck job). It targets hold pauses, which aren't the problem. Q-TD-139 question 1 now has a "no" recommendation, with this evidence.
- **Question 2 (starvation downs pausing holds):** keep as is. Starved downs are about a quarter and pauses aren't the bottleneck.
- **Q-TD-136 stays open.** The next useful measurement is why the hold never forms in s8/s9: which victory nodes the coalition lacks over time, and who takes them. Harness persona agency (bots giving orders) is still the main untested factor, since the stalled seeds are the ones where Static Offensives keep coming and the bots can't coordinate a push.
