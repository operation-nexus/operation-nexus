# SPEC: Threat Director (Enemy AI)

Status: Approved (owner, 2026-10-19) · Tracker: NX-155…NX-169, NX-309, NX-317 · ADR-0007 · Inputs: P63–71, C14–39

## 1. Purpose
Keep every population level (the harness crowd profiles of 0, 5, 50, 150, 300 and 500 viewers, Q-ALN-42) interesting and fair, with an enemy that seems to adapt but stays deterministic and testable.

## 2. Inputs (enemy view only, FOW-4)
- **Coalition power** `CP` = Σ over currently tracked coalition units (last-known tracks feed only Recon utility, TD-5; Q-FOW-14) of the unit's value, smoothed with an EMA over `director.cp.ema_window_s` (PROPOSED 300 s, game time, Q-ALN-23). Only sensed facts count: no supply readiness and no account age (Q-TD-2); alt-account abuse is handled by moderation outside the sim.
- Frontline state, node ownership, and enemy stockpiles.
- **Threat memory:** decaying counters per category: `convoy_attacks`, `radar_strikes`, `node_losses_by_sector`, `recon_losses_by_sector` (`sead_activity` joins with SEAD in P4). The half-life is configurable (`director.memory.half_life_s`, default 30 min of game time, Q-ALN-23).

## 3. Tiers (with hysteresis; replaces overlapping headcount bands)
| Tier | Enter when CP ≥ | Exit when CP < | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| PATTERN_OF_LIFE | — | — | Routine only: Recon, EscortConvoys, DecoyBlink and repositioning (DEFEND jobs at its own bases); Offensive only under the night progress cap (§5) |
| ALPHA | `director.tier.alpha_enter_cp` (0) | `alpha_enter_cp × exit_ratio` | Patrols, probes, supply maintenance, at most `director.max_ops.alpha` (1) operations at a time (the due cadence floor's capture operation may run one past it, ECONOMY EC-7, Q-TD-57) |
| BRAVO | `director.tier.bravo_enter_cp` | `bravo_enter_cp × director.tier.exit_ratio` (PROPOSED 0.8) | Concentrated offensives on weak sectors, IADS with overlapping SAMs |

**Escalation rule (Q-TD-1): provocation decides when, coalition power decides how far.** Provocation (§5a) maps to a level: ≥ `director.provocation.escalate_threshold` = ALPHA, ≥ `director.provocation.bravo_threshold` = BRAVO, otherwise Pattern of Life; a level drops only when provocation falls below its threshold × `director.tier.exit_ratio` (the same hysteresis as CP). The config loader requires `bravo_threshold > escalate_threshold`. CP maps to a ceiling by the table above. Each decision window the tier moves **at most one step** (default; Q-CFG-16 option `director.tier.max_step`) toward min(provocation level, CP ceiling), and only after the minimum dwell. A campaign starts in Pattern of Life (default; Q-CFG-16 option `director.tier.start`: a higher start has dwell 0 and the usual starting levels, so without provocation it settles back after one dwell; no `ThreatTierChanged` is emitted at campaign start, so a client that shows the tier reads it from the snapshot, not from the first event).

**CHARLIE is parked (Q-TD-3):** the named, telegraphed theater offensive (Q-GD-22, Q-ALN-35) waits for a larger roster and theaters; its rules are in GAMEPLAY-LATER.

There's a minimum dwell of 10 min of game time per tier (`director.tier.min_dwell_s`, Q-ALN-23). Tier changes are announced on the overlay and in a sitrep.

## 4. Decision loop (every 60 s of game time, `director.window_s`, Q-ALN-23)
1. Compute CP and the tier, and decay threat memory.
2. Budget for this window (ECONOMY EC-7, Q-TD-24, Q-TD-26): from the spend fraction of the Static bases' surplus stock (`director.spend_frac.<tier>`), plus a small share of the rear pool (`director.source_spend_frac`, spent on EC-13 terms), less the upkeep reserve (`director.upkeep_reserve_windows`), with a cadence floor (`director.cadence_windows`), never more than surplus plus rear pool. Every operation costs budget once; GP-7 only decides where its units spawn. **Guards (EC-8, Q-TD-12):** `sim.max_live_units_per_side` is a tick-rate guard only, and `director.max_spawns_per_window` stays as anti-spam. Raiding its convoys, stock or factories still starves the director (factories are parked, Q-GP-41). Formula, rear pool, reserve and cadence floor: [ECONOMY](ECONOMY.md). `director.budget.<tier>` and `director.max_live_units.<tier>` are removed by NX-412.
3. Generate candidate **operations** from the operation catalog (`content/operations.yaml`, CONFIG-AND-CONTENT §8a; Q-TD-7): Probe(sector), Recon(sector), Offensive(node), Retake(node), EscortConvoys(route), ReinforceIADS(sector) (first build: repair existing air-defense sites past Disabled; emission is DecoyBlink's; building new ones waits for GAMEPLAY-LATER §5a, Q-GP-46), StrikeBridge(node: a bridge on the coalition's lifeline, NX-401, §7a Part 5), DecoyBlink(radar). (TheaterOffensive waits with CHARLIE, Q-TD-3.) Each candidate has a cost and preconditions.
4. Score each candidate (Q-TD-6) = `director.op_priority.<op>` × target-value multiplier (`director.score.target_value.<node_kind>`, TBD) × weakness multiplier (from the target's defense-HP fraction as last seen in the enemy view; TBD curve) × threat-memory multiplier (`1 + counter` for the operation's matching counter, 1 when none matches) × proximity multiplier (Q-TD-4, below) × `director.score.victory_hold_mult` (Offensive and Retake on a node in a running victory hold, EC-9) × `director.score.income_node_mult` (Offensive and Retake on a coalition-held node that yields income, EC-12) + a `director` RNG noise term; tuned in the harness, refined in playtests. The noise is what makes it unpredictable but replayable. **Order (determinism):** candidates are enumerated sorted by (operation id, target id), one `director` draw is made per candidate in that order, and selection is greedy by (score descending, operation id, target id) within the budget.
5. **Adaptive defense (C37):** each threat-memory counter raises the utility of the matching defensive operation. Convoys are sent with no escort at first, then escorts appear as `convoy_attacks` grows, and drop away again as it decays. Each adaptation is announced with an INTEL line, at sector level only: it never names a site or updates a facility marker, so it reveals intent, not positions or states (Q-FOW-17, FOG-OF-WAR FOW-13).
6. Select within budget. Optionally, the LLM advisor picks among the top-K (§6).
7. **Spawning (Q-TD-8):** units bought from the budget spawn by the coalition's rule (GAMEPLAY GP-7, parity) for location only; their cost is the operation's budget, never a second charge, drawn once from the stock of the node where they spawn (ECONOMY EC-13): at the Static base nearest the job whose stock exceeds its starvation threshold by the forward-deploy margin, otherwise at the rear source behind its HQ.
8. **Turn operations into jobs** (Q-GD-34, Q-GD-37): the director is the strategic layer only. It never moves units directly (exception: `DecoyBlink` sets a site's emission flag directly; it is not a job, FOW-11); each selected operation becomes the jobs its **template** lists (Q-TD-7; e.g. Offensive(node) = stage at the nearest Static base, then strike the node's defenses, then send ground units to capture; EscortConvoys = a follow order on each convoy) on the Static's own Tasking Board (GAMEPLAY §1b), and Static units take them with the same mechanics as Quorum units: claim leases, feasibility check, priority aging, slots and dedupe keys, deterministic assignment order, job dependencies (Q-GD-37). Parity: the Static gets the same job types and mechanics, with no unique powers; only the director may compose them into richer combinations.

**TD-1 (time and pauses, Q-CFG-8, Q-OPS-8, Q-OPS-9, Q-ALN-2, Q-ALN-3, Q-ALN-23, Q-ALN-24, Q-ALN-35, Q-ALN-36):** every director timer runs inside the sim. These are **game time** (Q-ALN-23): the decision window (`director.window_s`), tier dwell (`director.tier.min_dwell_s`), the CP EMA window, the threat-memory and provocation half-lives, the PoL periods (`director.pol.*_period_s`) and the night-progress cap (`campaign.night_progress_cap_nodes_per_h`, per game hour). The advisor deadline is counted in decision windows, so it is game time too. Static units carrying out the director's jobs move in world time, like every unit. While the campaign is paused for any reason (`STAFF`, broadcast offline `TECHNICAL`, weekly `MAINTENANCE`, owner `CEASEFIRE`), none of them advances and the director makes no decision (SIM-CORE SC-11). A `STAFF_DIRECTOR_OVERRIDE` sent while paused is rejected with `CAMPAIGN_PAUSED` (Q-ALN-24), as are advisor choices and GM commands other than a GM release (Q-ALN-36). Taunt naming cooldowns (TD-4) and the GM idle auto-release (§6a) are **not** sim timers: they run on a monotonic real clock outside the sim (Q-ALN-4).

**TD-2 (own view only, FOW-4, Q-GD-37 item 9):** CP, provocation, candidate generation, scoring and the jobs the director posts are computed only from `EnemyView` (plus what the Static suffers). Operation-template selectors resolve against `EnemyView`, using discovered markers for facilities (FOW-13); a selector that resolves to nothing drops the operation. The Static's board never holds a job that targets or references an entity outside that view.

**TD-5 (scouting, Q-SIM-11, FOW-10):** `Recon(sector)` is an ordinary operation. It becomes jobs for ordinary ENEMY recon units (`ENEMY_RECON_UAV`, Q-TD-13) under parity (GAMEPLAY §0), paid from the same window budget, visible to viewers and killable. It adds no information beyond what those units' sensors detect, so the director's picture of a sector improves only while a scout lives. Its utility rises with the age of the director's last-known tracks over the sector (FOG-OF-WAR FOW-6a) and falls with recent scout losses there (threat memory), plus the `director` RNG noise term like any candidate. **A Recon operation targets a point, not a node (NX-379, Q-SIM-87):** the stalest cell of the sector's grid (`director.recon.grid_m`), where the Static's last-known picture is oldest or missing, that lies outside every known coalition air-defense ring whose last confirmed state is not Disabled, with a straight route from the base the scout is raised at that crosses none (the TB-2 test, GAMEPLAY §1b); no such point drops the sector that window, as an unresolved selector does. The scout loiters at the point for `director.recon.loiter_s` within `director.recon.loiter_radius_m`, then goes home. It reads the Static's view and discovered markers only.

## 4a. Forward staging (owner, 2026-10-25, NX-416; Q-TD-36, Q-TD-37, Q-TD-38)
The Static stages a ground attack at a forward base and projects it from there, instead of marching every capture column from its rear source. Viewers see the threat build: convoys run forward, units mass, then the attack goes in. The coalition can raid the staging base or its convoys to blunt it. It applies only with `economy.legacy_rules` off (ECONOMY EC-7, EC-13), so legacy behaviour and goldens are unchanged.
- **FS-1 When to stage.** An operation with a CAPTURE job (Offensive, Retake) on target T is staged when its capture kind's road travel time from the rear source to T exceeds `director.staging.direct_s`. Otherwise it spawns as EC-13 says.
- **FS-2 Staging base.** The staging base is the Static-held node, not a BRIDGE and with no capture bar, that can host the capture kind (EC-5 domain hosts) and has the shortest road travel time to T. Ties are broken by node id. If no held node is closer to T than the rear source, the operation is not staged.
- **FS-3 Stocking.** While an operation is staged, the staging base's forward-stock target (GAMEPLAY §5, Q-SIM-100) is raised to the cost of the operation's units that spawn there plus the base's starvation floor. The existing resupply jobs and the Director's convoys then carry stock there, and convoys prefer it. Nothing is teleported: stock moves only on trucks (Q-GP-16).
- **FS-4 Launch.** Each unit's cost is drawn only at the node it spawns at (EC-13; owner, Q-TD-38): never at a base the unit does not spawn from. The units of a kind the staging base can host spawn there and are paid there; a kind it cannot host (aircraft at an FOB) spawns as EC-13 says (`plan_deploy`'s base, else the rear source) and is paid at that node. A staged operation launches whole when every node it spawns at can pay for its own units; until then it waits. It waits at most `director.staging.max_wait_windows` decision windows; after that it launches by EC-13 from any node that can pay (the source included), or is dropped. At most `director.staging.max_staged` operations are staged at once. A staged operation counts against `director.max_ops.<tier>` and holds its budget share while it waits.
- **FS-5 Projection.** When staging starts, the Director emits `ThreatAdaptation{STAGING}` for the staging base's sector, at sector level only, never naming the base (FOW-13): "NULL forces are massing in <sector>". Units and convoys are seen only through fog of war, as any unit is. A re-stage (FS-6) emits the sitrep again only when the coalition currently tracks a live Static unit at the new base (owner, Q-TD-38: limited announcements under fog of war).
- **FS-6 Losing the base.** If the staging base is captured or contested, or its stock is raided below the cost when the wait runs out, the operation re-stages at the next base by FS-2 (the wait does not reset) or falls back by FS-4.
- **FS-7 State and determinism.** Staged operations (operation, target, base, start window) live in `DirectorState`, in the snapshot and hash. Candidates and bases are taken in id order. No new RNG draws.
- **FS-8 Tunable (EC-14).** `director.staging.direct_s`, `director.staging.max_wait_windows` and `director.staging.max_staged` are balance keys with full registry metadata and sweep ranges.
- **Parity (GAMEPLAY §0).** Staging composes existing mechanics: forward stock, convoys and the one spawn rule. The coalition stages the same way when players haul stock forward. The Director composing it is allowed by Q-GD-34; no new unit power.

## 5. Low-population behavior
At 0 humans, the coalition AI garrison holds the line and the Static drops to **Pattern of Life** (§5a); the campaign clock keeps running: it never pauses for lack of humans (Q-GD-4 amended); only a pause with a reason stops it (TD-1, Q-ALN-3). With 0 humans the Static can never take the coalition's `forward_airbase` or bring the sanctuary below 1 defense HP (GAMEPLAY §9, Q-GP-28). Enemy progress is capped at `campaign.night_progress_cap_nodes_per_h` (PROPOSED 1 node per 6 h of game time, Q-ALN-23). **"No humans" means no live viewer unit, the same test as the idle floor (Q-GP-44, Q-SIM-85).** Every Static flip stamps `DirectorState.last_flip` (in the snapshot and hash), with or without viewers (Q-SIM-86). While there are none, a Static capture flip is allowed only if none happened in the last 1 ÷ the cap game hours; until then the capture bar holds one step short of the flip, the node is recorded in `DirectorState.held`, and the Director launches no capture operation. **A held bar stays held until the cap frees, even if a viewer arrives**, so a new viewer does not see held bases fall at once (§5a: 0 to 1 viewer changes nothing until that operator acts); coalition units can still contest it as any bar, and it leaves `held` when it is gone or drained. A bar that had not reached the flip when the viewer arrived is not held and flips as usual. The idle floor (the forward airbase, the sanctuary's last HP) is unchanged. While the CP ceiling is ALPHA, a proximity multiplier (`director.score.provocation_proximity`, TBD) favours operations in `last_provocation_sector` (director state, in the snapshot and hash; the sector of the most recent provocation the Static sensed, ties broken by sector id), so a lone viewer harassing one sector gets a local fight there while the rest of the map stays calm (Q-TD-4; never by headcount, TS-1).

## 5a. Pattern of Life (owner requirement, 2026-10-17)
When nobody is playing, **and when viewers first arrive**, the Static mounts no offensives beyond the night progress cap (Q-GP-44). It runs a believable **Pattern of Life (PoL)** that scouts can observe and learn from:
- **Routine scouting (Q-TD-2 owner note):** even with no provocation, the director keeps sending Recon operations (TD-5) on a PoL cadence, so it is always looking.
- **Maintenance only, never a swarm (Q-TD-11 owner note):** at Pattern of Life the director only replaces losses up to each base's garrison quota from the theater file (`garrison_quota` per base); it never grows its force net, however long the war is quiet. Its convoys at Pattern of Life carry only what that upkeep needs.
- **Garrison upkeep (Q-SIM-85).** A routine catalog operation (`GARRISON_UPKEEP`, every tier, outside `director.max_ops.<tier>`): a Static base with fewer live `ENEMY_DIRECTOR` units homed there (including any away on jobs) than its `garrison_quota` gets one replacement per window, raised at the base, paid from surplus like any operation. **The bases start the campaign at their quota** (free, homed, the default kinds in turn, as part of setup; Q-SIM-86), so early viewers do not find empty bases. The kind comes from `director.garrison.kinds.<node kind>`, used round-robin; `director.garrison.replace_per_window` (1) units are bought per short base per window. Retirement (Q-SIM-84) takes only units above their home base's quota, highest id first, so a garrison sitting at its base is never churned. **A garrison unit defends its own base** (the board gives it a DEFEND of that base, e.g. against a capture bar) **and nothing else by itself**: no other board job, and an operation never reuses it. Only the Director sends it away, for a patrol; an idle one away from its held home is sent back (Q-SIM-86). Default kinds: AIRBASE ENEMY_FIGHTER and ENEMY_ARMOR in turn, HQ and FOB ENEMY_ARMOR; a garrison kind must be raised at its base kind and an aircraft needs a landing base (the loader refuses the rest).
- **Patrols (Q-SIM-85).** A `PATROL` job on the Static's board. Every `director.pol.patrol_period_s`, each Static base with idle ground garrison units homed there posts patrols until `director.pol.patrols_per_base` (1) are out: out to the base's road neighbour toward the frontline (the nearest node within `director.frontline_hops` hops that the Static does not hold, neutral or not; never a node the coalition holds; with no front in reach, the nearest such neighbour), then home; DONE on return. A garrison unit (one with a home base) and a unit on a PATROL job never start or advance a capture bar (Q-SIM-86): a garrison only defends, so a patrol can turn at a neutral node. A patrol that fails (its home or turning node is lost) sends its unit to the nearest friendly base; with no route that avoids the coalition's nodes the unit stands, and it retires once idle. A job takes `director.pol.patrol_slots` (1) units. Patrols are the visible garrison rotation; no separate rotation is built.
- **Convoys (Q-SIM-85).** Every `director.pol.convoy_period_s`, up to `director.pol.convoys_per_period` (1) `RESUPPLY` jobs, loaded by the usual reload rule (GAMEPLAY §1b, Q-GP-24: the nearest Static base with stock above its floor, the rear source only when none has; NX-389, Q-SIM-102: on a run to a base that is not starving, has no damaged facility and is below its forward stock target, a source counts only its stock above its own target, so such a convoy usually loads at the rear source), one each to the Static bases with the largest upkeep shortfall (the cost of its missing garrison units plus the floor less its stock, so a base below its floor counts too; Q-SIM-86); none when no base falls short. A truck carries at most `logistics.truck_cargo`. A new carrier costs `director.pol.convoy_cost` (0) from the surplus; the stock it moves is accounted as cargo.
- **Night progress (Q-SIM-85).** At Pattern of Life (default; Q-CFG-16 option `director.pol.capture_slot_tiers`) the Director may launch up to `director.pol.offensives_per_period` (1) capture operations (any catalog operation with a CAPTURE job: Offensive, Retake) per `director.pol.offensive_period_s`, outside the window budget but paid from the real surplus, and never while the night cap (§5) is spent.
- **No offensives from the board (Q-SIM-85).** The Static's board generates STRIKE jobs against discovered sites only from `tasking.static_offense_min_tier` (ALPHA) up; at Pattern of Life it still posts DEFEND, RESUPPLY (including stocking jobs while it has a live carrier, NX-389), scout and the Director's PATROL jobs, and the Director's own operations (the capture slot) post their jobs themselves. The coalition's board is not gated.
- **Scout intercepts (Q-SIM-96, NX-382; in code).** The Static hunts coalition scouts it can see, but not instantly. When the Static's view (TD-2, FOW: tracks only, never ground truth) holds a coalition aircraft of a painter kind within `director.intercept.radius_m` (PROPOSED 30,000 m) of a Static-held node, continuously for `director.intercept.reaction_s` (PROPOSED 60 game s) at ALPHA and above, or for `director.intercept.pol_linger_s` (PROPOSED 600 game s) at Pattern of Life, its board posts `intercept:<entity>` for armed AIR units that can hit it, priority `tasking.priority.intercept` (PROPOSED 45: below DEFEND 60, above STRIKE 40), slots `tasking.slots.intercept` (PROPOSED 1). Interceptors are ordinary Static units that launch from a base, so a scout that leaves in time escapes. A garrison fighter (one with a home, Q-SIM-85) may take an intercept within `director.intercept.leash_m` (PROPOSED 80,000 m) of its home and returns home after; this is the one exception to garrison units taking only DEFEND jobs at home. The job is done when the scout is destroyed, has not been tracked for `director.intercept.lost_s` (PROPOSED 120 game s), or is no longer within the radius of any Static-held node. **An intercept never adds provocation** (only people provoke, Q-TD-11): a scout flying in is not an escalation, only what its commander does (THREAT-DIRECTOR §5a). The job's lifecycle events (`JobCreated`, `JobClaimed`, ...) are what the overlay can call out ("BANDITS SCRAMBLED"); the callout itself is OVERLAY work. **In code now (NX-382 part 2):** key `intercept:<entity>` (`JobKind` `INTERCEPT`, `Job.entity` the scout, `Job.pos` the Static's track of it, `JobCreated.entity_id`), urgency 1.0, `tasking_gen._intercept`. Interpretations: the reaction clock is `World.intercept_seen` (scout id to the board pass that first saw it), updated at every Static board pass, so continuity is sampled each `tasking.reeval_every_ticks` and the clock starts at the first pass that sees the scout; an untracked pass, a last-known track or a track outside every held node's radius drops its entry and the wait starts over; the wait is read at posting, by the tier then (ALPHA's reaction up, Pattern of Life's linger below). "A painter kind" is `rules.painters` aircraft (the recon). Interceptors are armed aircraft of any Static owner (the Director's units, garrison included) with a round of a weapon that reaches the scout's domain and default altitude band (`tasking_gen.can_hit`); a garrison unit's leash holds for the whole chase: it is checked against the scout's current track when the unit takes the job and at every board pass after, and a garrison claimant whose scout's track passes the leash is released (`JobReleased`) and stops, and the ordinary recall takes it home. A scout that is gone or Disabled is never hunted (a liveness read of the truth, like the job's DONE; its contact stays tracked until sensing drops it), so a kill is not followed by a new job. A taker flies to the track position (never the scout's true position, FOW), re-aimed each board pass; it is released to the board when the job ends, with its path cleared, and an idle garrison unit away from home is then recalled by the ordinary rule (Q-SIM-86). Slots follow the ordinary rule, so with every job full a second fighter may overfill (`tasking.overfill_factor`). Outcome: DONE when the scout is destroyed or Disabled (it needs an active claimant, else the usual EXPIRED), EXPIRED when the track is lost for `lost_s` (this holds for a claimed job; an unclaimed one is dropped as soon as its scout is no longer tracked, as no longer generated) (`Job.lost` is the tick it was first found untracked; a track's age is counted back) or its last track leaves every held node's radius; there is no FAILED. Nothing in `provocation` counts an intercept: a shot by a Static unit at a coalition scout is not a provocation hit (the victim is not the Static's) and the job's posting, claiming and ending note nothing.
- Radar on/off cycles are DecoyBlink (FOW-11; the proximity rule is already built, NX-156).
- **Repositioning between bases:** staging aircraft and ground units toward forward bases, refuel/rearm stops. Staging is how attacks are prepared, so a sharp scout can see an offensive coming (only within coalition sensor coverage or as SIGINT blobs, FOG-OF-WAR).

**Escalation is triggered by provocation, not headcount** (GAMEPLAY TS-1):
- A decaying `provocation` score rises with coalition actions the Static **senses or suffers**: losses or damage to its units, convoys and facilities, node flips, recon of its rear only when its sensors track the scout, and channel-point redemptions (TD-2, Q-FOW-15: nothing unsensed counts). **Only people provoke (Q-TD-11):** only actions by viewer units under player control count, plus redemptions; AI garrison, facility and AUTO actions add 0, so the Static never escalates against an empty stream.
- **Redemption weights (Q-AI-9, Q-ALN-13):** every redemption type has its own weight, `director.provocation.weights.redemption.<type>` (admin-tunable). There is **no offensive redemption class**: no redemption may deal damage (SUPPORT-REDEMPTIONS §4). Classes: support (supply drop, respawn skip, reserve-queue jump) = small weight; cosmetic (color, ping, shoutout, spectacle) = 0. Weight values are PROPOSED / TBD by harness.
- PoL → ALPHA → BRAVO follows the escalation rule in §3 (Q-TD-1).
- Going from 0 viewers to 1 changes **nothing** until that operator acts. They get time to observe and plan.
- With no provocation, the enemy decays back to PoL. Night-progress caps still apply (§5).

Config keys (CONFIG §6.7, all PROPOSED / TBD by harness): `director.pol.patrol_period_s`, `director.pol.convoy_period_s`, `director.pol.offensive_period_s`, `director.pol.convoy_kind`, `director.pol.patrols_per_base`, `director.pol.convoys_per_period`, `director.pol.offensives_per_period`, `director.pol.convoy_cost`, `director.garrison.kinds.<node kind>`, `director.garrison.replace_per_window`, `tasking.static_offense_min_tier`, `campaign.night_progress_cap_nodes_per_h`, `director.intercept.radius_m`, `director.intercept.reaction_s`, `director.intercept.pol_linger_s`, `director.intercept.leash_m`, `director.intercept.lost_s`, `tasking.priority.intercept`, `tasking.slots.intercept`, `director.provocation.weights.*` (including `.redemption.<type>`), `director.provocation.half_life_s`, `director.provocation.escalate_threshold`, `director.provocation.bravo_threshold`, `director.op_priority.<op>`, `director.score.*`. Routine Recon at Pattern of Life is scored like any operation; there is no separate cadence key.

## 5b. Active defense (owner, 2026-10-26, NX-424; Q-TD-44)
Status: Draft (needs owner sign-off before code) · Tracker: NX-424 · Inputs: Q-TD-44 (G1 to G4 and the owner's "real threats only" rule), Q-SIM-96, Q-TD-46, ECONOMY EC-3, FOG-OF-WAR FOW-4 and FOW-8

**Purpose.** A side's AI answers a real attack on one of its nodes with units it already has. Fighters go after attacking aircraft; armor reinforces the node against attacking ground units. Nothing is spawned and nothing is bought. Viewers see a base fight back, so a strike on a Static base meets its idle fighters, and a careless strike package pays for it. The Static and the coalition's AI garrison use the same rule and the same keys (G4, EC-3).

This section composes existing mechanics: the Static's Tasking Board, the scout-intercept job and leash (§5a, Q-SIM-96), DEFEND jobs (GAMEPLAY §1b) and the garrison recall (Q-SIM-86). It adds no unit power (GAMEPLAY §0). Values in this section are PROPOSED. Readings marked "lead reading" are open below.

**Seams in code today (for the builder; read-only facts).**
- The scout intercept is the model. `tasking_gen.watched` picks the tracked contacts near held nodes and reads the truth only for liveness (`services/sim/src/nexus/core/tasking_gen.py:196-209`). `tasking._watch` keeps its clock in `World.intercept_seen` before each Static pass (`tasking.py:404-405`, `tasking.py:423-427`). `tasking_gen._intercept` posts `intercept:<entity>` once the wait has passed (`tasking_gen.py:499-512`).
- A chase follows the side's track, never the true position, and releases a garrison claimant past its leash (`tasking.py:539-569`, `_in_leash` and `_chase`). The job ends DONE on a kill, else EXPIRED when lost or out of range (`tasking.py:572-581`, `tasking.py:651-655`). At its end an aircraft stops where it is (`tasking.py:700-701`).
- DEFEND is generated for a held, capturable node with a tracked enemy GROUND unit inside `tasking.defend_radius_m` (10,000 m, `balance/default.yaml:177`), with no armed or closing test (`tasking_gen.py:382-404`). It ends DONE when a claimant stands at the node and the node is no longer menaced, FAILED when the node is lost (`tasking.py:600-626`).
- A garrison unit (`Entity.home`, `world.py:328-330`) is given only a DEFEND of its home or an intercept within its leash (`tasking.py:897-908`). An idle garrison unit away from its home is sent back at the next decision window (`launch.py:803-814`). An operation never reuses a garrison unit (`launch.py:817-822`).
- "Idle" for the board means no job, no order, no path, not Disabled, not RTB, not grounded (`tasking_gen.py:212-219`). Assignment takes idle units in id order, best job first (`tasking.py:911-937`).
- The coalition's AI garrison in code today is its facilities only: setup spawns them as `AI_GARRISON`, STATIC domain (`services/sim/src/nexus/runtime/campaign_config.py:243-254`). The Static's garrison is mobile and homed (`campaign_config.py:225-240`). The coalition has no mobile AI unit yet.

**AD-1 Threat (G1, owner rule).** A *threat* to a side is an enemy unit that meets all of these at a board pass:
1. It is in the side's `SideView` as a current track (`Contact.age` is None, `world.py:368-378`). A last-known track, a SIGINT blob or a discovered marker never counts (TD-2, FOW-4).
2. Its kind is armed: it is in `rules.armed`, the kinds whose catalog lists weapons (`rules.py:1094`). It is the catalog, not the unit's live ammo, which is hidden state (as escorts read it, GAMEPLAY §1b, Q-SIM-76).
3. Its domain is AIR or GROUND. STATIC facilities never move, so they are never threats.
4. It is not a scout the intercept rule watches: a painter aircraft (`rules.painters`) stays under §5a only. Today both painter kinds are unarmed (`content/units/recon-uav.yaml:20`, `content/units/enemy-recon-uav.yaml:20`), so rule 2 already excludes them. Rule 4 keeps an armed painter added later from drawing two jobs.
5. It is still alive and not Disabled: a liveness read of the truth, the same exception `watched` makes, so a wreck that stays tracked is not answered.
6. It is **closing** on a node the side holds (AD-2).

Unarmed units (scouts, trucks, convoys) never count. A unit passing by, loitering or leaving is not a threat.

**AD-2 Closing (owner pick, 2026-10-26).** Closing is measured per pair (enemy unit, node the side holds), only at board passes (every `tasking.reeval_every_ticks`, 50 ticks, `balance/default.yaml:149`):
- At each pass, for each pair where the unit meets AD-1 rules 1 to 5 and its track lies within `director.defense.radius_m` of the node, the side records a sample (pass tick, range). Range is the straight-line distance from the track's position to the node's position, in metres (`math.hypot`, as `held_within` and `_defend` compute it).
- A pair whose unit is not tracked at a pass, is tracked only as last-known, or is outside the radius at a pass, loses all its samples. The clock starts over (as the intercept clock does).
- Samples older than the window are dropped. The **window start** is the newest sample whose tick is at or before `now − reaction`, where `reaction` is `director.defense.reaction_s` in ticks, rounded up. Every sample after it is kept.
- The pair is **closing** at this pass when it has a window-start sample, so it was continuously in the radius for at least `reaction`, and `range_now ≤ range_start − director.defense.closing_min_m`.
- `closing_min_m` exists so a unit circling the node at a steady range, or drifting by a few metres, does not count. It is a proposed key (open question 1). With it at 0 any shrink counts.
- This is a sliding window (lead reading, open question 2): it compares the range now with the range `reaction` ago, so a unit that loitered for ten minutes and then edges in counts only once it has closed by the margin over the last `reaction`.

A unit closing on several nodes is one threat. Its **target node** is the one with the smallest `range_now`, ties by node id.

Known limit, accepted by the owner's rule: a unit flying a straight line past a node closes during the first half of its pass. It can become a threat before it turns away. When it then leaves, an unclaimed job is dropped at once (AD-4), and a claimed one ends by the AD-5 rules.

**AD-3 Responders (G2).** A responder is a unit of the side that is all of these:
- **Owned by the side's AI.** For the Static: `ENEMY_DIRECTOR`. For the coalition: `AI_GARRISON` (open question 9). A viewer's unit is never a responder.
- **Idle by the board's test** (`tasking_gen.is_idle`), so it is not Disabled, not RTB, not grounded (ECONOMY EC-6) and holds no job. Units on an operation's job (STRIKE, CAPTURE, ESCORT_CONVOY, RESUPPLY, SCOUT, PATROL, INTERCEPT) are never pulled off it. A unit holding an arrived STAGE claim is the open case (open question 4).
- **Within its leash, if it has a home.** A garrison unit responds only when the anchor point lies within `director.defense.leash_m` of its home. For an air threat the anchor is the threat's track (checked when it takes the job and at every pass after, as `_chase` does for intercepts). For a ground threat the anchor is the target node (fixed, checked when it takes the job). A unit with no home has no leash, as `_in_leash` reads it today (open question 5). Its own feasibility rules still bound it (fuel and bingo, road route, ring crossing).
- **The right tool for the threat.** For an **air threat**: an AIR unit with a round that can hit the threat's domain and altitude band (`tasking_gen.can_hit`, `tasking_gen.py:172-182`), so fighters intercept. For a **ground threat**: a GROUND unit that can hit it, so armor reinforces. Aircraft do not answer ground threats (G2 as written; open question 12).
- **Not already the node's own defender.** For a ground threat, a unit homed at the target node is not a responder: it already takes that node's `defend:<node>` job. For an air threat there is no such job, so the target node's own garrison fighters do respond. This is the acceptance case (NX-424): "a tracked strike group near a Static base draws its idle fighters".

**AD-4 The response job.** The side's board generates one job per threat, in the generate step (`tasking_gen.generate`, `tasking_gen.py:257-273`), from the side's view only (TB-9, TD-2):
- Key `respond:<entity>` (name PROPOSED), added to the board's `GENERATED` prefixes (`tasking_gen.py:99-109`). `Job.entity` is the threat. `JobCreated.entity_id` names it, which FOW-8 allows because the side tracks it.
- **Air threat:** kind `INTERCEPT`. `Job.pos` is the side's track of the threat, re-aimed at every pass, never its true position. Claimants fly to the track (the `_chase` rule). Priority `tasking.priority.intercept` (45: below DEFEND, above STRIKE).
- **Ground threat:** kind `DEFEND`. `Job.node` is the target node. Claimants drive there by road and hold it, engaging by COMBAT's ordinary rules. Priority `tasking.priority.defend` (60).
- Urgency 1.0. Slots `director.defense.max_responders`. **Overfill never applies** (as for a Recon point job, `tasking.py:290-298`), so the cap is hard: at most `max_responders` claimants per threat at any time.
- The board posts it only while the side has a live unit that could be a responder for it (AD-3). For the coalition this is narrower than the usual staffing rule, which also counts roles a viewer could JOIN (`tasking_gen.py:242-248`).
- **Generation stops when the threat is no longer closing.** By the board's existing rule an unclaimed generated job that is not generated again expires at once (`tasking.py:414-417`). A claimed job stays until its outcome (AD-5). So a strike package that arrives and orbits over the base keeps its responders (lead reading, open question 3) but draws no new job.
- With `max_responders` at 0, nothing is generated (the off switch, open question 14).

**AD-5 Release and return (leash).**
- **DONE** when the threat is destroyed or Disabled and the job has a participant, else EXPIRED (the intercept rule).
- **EXPIRED** when the side has not tracked the threat for `director.defense.lost_s` (`Job.lost`, as intercepts count it), or when its last track is no longer within `director.defense.radius_m` of any node the side holds.
- **FAILED** (ground job only) when the side loses the target node (the DEFEND rule).
- **Leash.** A garrison claimant of an air job whose threat's track passes its leash is released (`JobReleased`) and stops, as in `_chase`.
- **Afterwards.** An aircraft stops where it is when the job ends, and a ground unit stays at the node. Each is then an ordinary idle unit. A garrison unit away from its home is recalled at the next decision window (`launch._recall`). A unit with no home may be reused by a later operation or retired after `director.idle_retire_s` (Q-SIM-84). A responder is not idle while it holds the job, so it is not retired during a response.
- A responder that loses its claim (lost, Disabled, sent home at bingo) frees its slot. Another responder may take the slot, still within the cap.

**AD-6 Operations, staging and the hold response.**
- **Not an operation.** Active defense is board work, not a Director operation. It has no catalog entry, does not count against `director.max_ops.<tier>` or `director.max_spawns_per_window`, does not stamp `DirectorState.last_operation`, and is never staged (§4a).
- **Operations keep their units.** Responders are idle, so no operation loses a unit on a job. An operation's reuse of idle units (`launch._reusable`) cannot take a unit while it responds. Garrison units are never reused, as today.
- **Staging (§4a).** A staged operation in `DirectorState.staged` has no units yet, so it is unaffected. The staging base's stock target is unaffected. Units holding a STAGE claim: open question 4.
- **Hold response (Q-TD-46, NX-430).** Independent. The hold response's capture operations put the Static's units on CAPTURE and STRIKE jobs, so they are never responders. When the coalition has mobile AI garrison units, the Static's hold-response jets closing on a hold node are threats to the coalition under AD-1, by the same rule (G4).
- **Scout intercept (§5a).** Unchanged. A scout never draws a `respond:` job (AD-1 rule 4). A unit on an intercept is not idle, so it is never pulled.
- **Escorts.** Units following a convoy hold a job or a follow order, so they are not responders.

**AD-7 Cost and budget.** A response spawns nothing and buys nothing:
- No `UnitSpawned`, no `SupplyConsumed{SPAWN}`, no draw on the window budget (step 2), the rear account or the pool.
- Responders spend fuel and rounds, which the ordinary refuel and rearm rules restock at a landing base from its stock (GAMEPLAY §5b). That is the only cost, and it already applies to any sortie.
- **Upkeep (EC-4 to EC-6) is unchanged.** One side effect: a responder holds a claimed job, so it is *busy* for the payment order (EC-6) and is paid before idle units at its home.
- **No provocation and no threat memory from responding.** A Static responder's hits land on coalition units, which is not a provocation (the victim is not the Static's, §7a). A coalition AI garrison's hits on Static units add `director.provocation.ai_shooter_mult` × the weight, 0 by default (`services/sim/src/nexus/core/provocation.py:8-10`, `provocation.py:102-103`). Posting, claiming and ending a `respond:` job note nothing.

**AD-8 The coalition's AI garrison (G4, EC-3).** The same rule, the same keys and the same job, generated on the coalition's board from the coalition's view.
- Only `AI_GARRISON` units respond (AD-3). This is defense of its own nodes, so it does not break "the AI garrison never initiates offense" (GAMEPLAY §1b). A DEFEND is already in `tasking.ai_jobs`, and `_may_take` already lets an AI unit take an INTERCEPT by itself (`tasking.py:308-314`).
- **Inert in code until the coalition has mobile AI units.** Its AI garrison is facilities today, which cannot move. The leash needs a home, and `Entity.home` is refused on a unit that is not the Director's (§7a Part 3 load checks). Open question 10.
- Viewer units neither take nor see `respond:` jobs as their own work (open question 9). They keep the ordinary DEFEND and NEEDED list.

**AD-9 Fog of war (FOW-4, FOW-8, TD-2).** Detection, closing, the target node and the job's position read only the side's view: current tracks and node positions and owners. The one read of the truth is liveness (AD-1 rule 5), the same exception the scout intercept makes. Responders chase the track, never the true position. A threat the side does not track draws nothing. Two worlds that differ only in an untracked enemy unit give the same board. No sitrep is added (G3). The job's lifecycle events (`JobCreated`, `JobClaimed`, `JobDone`, ...) are what the overlay may call out; the callout is OVERLAY work.

**AD-10 Determinism and state.**
- Order: the boards run COALITION then ENEMY, as today (`tasking.py:383-420`). For each side the samples are updated after settling and before generation, in the slot where `_watch` runs for the Static. Pairs are taken in (entity id, node id) order, threats in entity id order. The target node is the smallest range, ties by node id. Responders are assigned by the board's existing rule: idle units in id order, jobs in rank order, equal-priority runs ordered by one `tasking` draw each (SC-5).
- No new RNG stream and no draw in detection or generation. With responders on, the board holds more jobs, so the `tasking` draws in equal-priority runs change and goldens change. With `max_responders` 0 nothing is posted and no draw changes, so goldens are unchanged.
- Ranges are floats from `math.hypot` on track positions, compared directly, as `_defend` and `held_within` already do. The same inputs give the same result on one thread (SIM-CORE).
- **State:** the closing samples, for example `World.defense_seen` (side → (entity id, node id) → ascending list of (pass tick, range)), are world state, in the snapshot and the hash, with a snapshot format bump. Restore refuses: a tick past the clock, a sample list not ascending by tick, a node that is not a theater node, and an entity id that does not exist. There is no upper bound tied to `reaction_s`, because a hot reload may change it (as with `blink_ready`, §7a Part 2). On a hot reload the window simply uses the new value at the next pass.
- **Time (TD-1).** Board passes never run while frozen (SC-11), so a pause freezes the samples. `reaction_s` and `lost_s` are game time, counted in running ticks, so `sim.time_scale` changes no count.
- **Size.** At most one sample list per (tracked enemy unit, held node in radius), each at most `reaction / reeval + 1` samples long at the shipped values (13 at 60 s and 50 ticks). The pass cost must stay inside SC-10; a perf test checks it (below).

**AD-12 Tests (acceptance).**
- **NX-424 acceptance.** A seeded world: two coalition STRIKE_FIGHTERs that the Static tracks fly straight at a Static AIRBASE whose garrison fighters are idle. After `reaction_s` the Static's board posts one `respond:<id>` INTERCEPT per jet. The garrison fighters claim them, at most `max_responders` per jet. The run emits no `UnitSpawned` and no `SupplyConsumed{SPAWN}` from it, and the window budget and every stock are the same as in a run with `max_responders` 0, before the first shot.
- **Real threats only.** No job is posted for: a jet flying a constant-range circle round the base; a jet flying away; a jet that closes by less than `closing_min_m` over the window; a RECON_UAV or a LOGI_TRUCK closing (the scout still gets its intercept, §5a); a unit tracked only as last-known.
- **Continuity.** One pass with the unit untracked, or outside the radius, resets the clock: the job comes `reaction` after it is tracked again, not before.
- **Sliding window.** A jet that loiters at 25 km for 10 minutes and then holds still draws nothing. When it then closes by the margin over one window, it draws a job at that pass.
- **Fog (TD-2).** Two worlds that differ only in a closing coalition jet the Static does not track give the same board and the same events. Every `respond:` job references a tracked entity.
- **Cap.** Five idle fighters and one threat with `max_responders` 2: never more than 2 claimants, also when every other job is full (no overfill).
- **Leash.** A garrison fighter whose home is farther than `leash_m` from the track does not take the job. One that took it is released when the track passes the leash, stops, and is recalled home at the next window.
- **Ground.** A coalition GARRISON_INFANTRY closing on a Static FOB draws a `respond:<id>` DEFEND taken by idle armor homed at a neighbouring base within the leash. Armor homed at the FOB takes `defend:<fob>`, not the response. The job FAILS when the FOB falls.
- **Never pulled.** Units on STRIKE, CAPTURE, ESCORT_CONVOY, PATROL, SCOUT, RESUPPLY or INTERCEPT jobs, grounded units and RTB units never take a `respond:` job.
- **Outcomes.** DONE when the threat is destroyed or Disabled with a participant. EXPIRED after `lost_s` untracked, or when the track leaves every held node's radius. An unclaimed job is dropped at the first pass at which the threat is no longer closing.
- **No side effects.** Responding adds no provocation and no threat memory, launches no operation and leaves `last_operation`, `max_ops` and staging unchanged.
- **Parity (EC-3).** A mirrored hand-built world where the coalition has an `AI_GARRISON` fighter with a home gives the mirrored jobs and claims. A viewer's unit never takes a `respond:` job.
- **Determinism.** Same seed, same events. A snapshot taken mid-window and restored gives the same events. A pause freezes the samples. With `sim.time_scale` ≠ 1 the job comes after the same number of ticks. With `max_responders` 0 the goldens are byte-identical to the build before.
- **Load checks.** A restore refuses each AD-10 case.
- **Perf.** A pass with 500 enemy units tracked near held nodes stays inside SC-10 (marker `perf`).

## 6a. Game Master takeover (owner requirement)
The owner, or an admin with the `GAME_MASTER` permission, can take over the director (ADMIN-RBAC spec):
- **Scope (Q-AI-8, Q-ALN-10):** a GM drives the Static and triggers world events (TS-1), nothing else. World events may benefit the coalition (supply drop, weather clearing, friendly convoy) but must be visible and in-fiction. A GM never grants units, ammo or XP to specific players. Campaign setup (objectives, unlimited ammo and other campaign options) is owner/Admin only, never GM (GAMEPLAY §9c).
- **Job injection (Q-GD-37 item 11, Q-ALN-11):** a GM injects jobs into the **Static's board only**. An injection that targets the Quorum's board is rejected.
- **Modes (Q-TD-9):**
  - **ASSIST** (first GM release): the GM injects or vetoes operations while the rules keep running.
  - **FULL** (the next release after ASSIST; the owner wants it soon): the rules pause, and the GM issues operations from the same operation catalog.
- **Constraints:** GM operations are still validated against the tier budget and preconditions. A GM *may* exceed the budget only with an explicit `override_budget` flag, which is audited.
- **Logging:** every GM action is a logged `STAFF_*` command, so replays stay deterministic (ADR-0002), and is flagged as a GM action in replays (Q-AI-8).
- **On stream:** a "COMMAND: GAME MASTER" badge is always visible while a human GM is in control, on every layout including 9:16 (Q-ALN-17; DATA-INTEGRITY DI-5: never disguise human control as AI or vice versa).

A live operation (a job with key `op:<op>:<target>:...` on the board) is not posted twice, and a tier runs at most `director.max_ops.<tier>` operations with jobs at a time (ALPHA 1, BRAVO none; null is no cap; Pattern of Life has no key, its operations are all routine), not counting the routine ones (those enabled at Pattern of Life, derived from the catalog's tiers; Q-SIM-84); the due cadence floor's capture operation may run one past the cap (`select`'s `over`, Q-TD-57). Score = `director.op_priority` x target value (`score.target_value.<node kind>` for a node of the other side, else 1) x weakness (1 at full defense HP, linear to `score.weakness_max` 2.0 at none, from the view's defense HP over the node's maximum) x memory (RAISE: `1 + c`, c in events, undecayed counter / 1,000,000; LOWER: `1 / (1 + c)`) x `score.provocation_proximity` (a target in `last_provocation_sector` while the CP ceiling is one of `score.proximity_tiers`, default ALPHA) x, for RECON, `1 + s` (TD-5: s is 0 with a current track in the sector, `age / track_fade` of its oldest last-known track, 1 with none). Noise: one `director` draw per candidate, `score x (1 + noise_scale x r / 1000)` with r in [-1000, 1000]. Selection is greedy by (score descending, operation id, target id) within the window budget, `max_spawns_per_window` and the room below `max_live_units.<tier>`; an escort operation shrinks to the convoys that fit.
- **Pay and spawn (steps 2, 7; Q-TD-8, Q-TD-12).** The operation's cost is debited from the surplus of the Static's finite-stock bases (the base its first unit spawned at first, then by id), as `SupplyConsumed{SPAWN}`, one per base; units cost nothing more, so the budget is the stock. One unit per template slot, kinds in turn, from an idle live Director unit of the kind first (lowest id; nothing spawned or paid for it: the cost is scaled by the units actually spawned, Q-SIM-84), else a new one, at `plan_deploy`'s base for the unit's job (the nearest Static base with surplus over the forward-deploy margin, else the rear source), full of fuel and rounds, owner `ENEMY_DIRECTOR`, planned on the stock before the debit.
- **Payment rule.** The window's budget is a plan, not a reservation: operations launched earlier in the window (their carriers load on taking a job), garrison upkeep, Pattern of Life convoys and the capture slot draw on the same surplus. So each operation is checked at launch against the real payable surplus (the same finite-stock bases `_pay` debits) for the units it will actually spawn, before it posts or spawns anything; if the surplus cannot pay the full cost the operation is skipped whole (no jobs, no units, no partial payment, no INTEL announcement). `_pay` still raises if it is ever asked for more than exists: that is an invariant, not a path.
- **Jobs (step 8).** `post_job` per template job in order, each depending on the last job of the step before; the units claim at spawn. A job still waiting keeps its claimants' leases; when the job before is DONE the dependant's expiry clock restarts and its claimants set off. `STAGE`: go to the base and hold, DONE when every slot is claimed and every claimant is there, FAILED if the base falls. `CAPTURE`: go to the node and stay, DONE when the node is the side's, EXPIRED at its expiry even with claimants (the clock restarts while a claimant is still driving). A job another waits for is DONE when its goal is met even with no live participant. A selector resolves as: `target` and `target_defenses` the target node, `nearest_own_base` the side's base nearest the target, `sector_point` the Recon point of the sector (Part 4), `each_convoy` one job per convoy (`ESCORT_CONVOY`, a follow order on the truck).
- **DecoyBlink (Q-SIM-82).** The radar at the target base goes dark for `director.decoy_blink.dark_s` (120 game s) and then emits again; the same radar cannot blink again for `director.decoy_blink.cooldown_s` (600 game s, from when it went dark); at most one radar blinks per window. Directly: no job, no unit, 0 cost. `DirectorState.dark` (base -> tick it resumes) and `blink_ready` (base -> tick it may blink again) are in the snapshot and hash.
- **INTEL (step 5).** `ThreatAdaptation{CONVOY_ESCORT | IADS_REINFORCED, sector, UP | DOWN}`: UP when an operation whose RAISE counter is `convoy_attacks` or `radar_strikes` launches with that counter above 0 (once per category); DOWN when the counter has decayed below `director.adaptation_down_below` (1 event). Sector level only. Operation jobs have urgency `director.job_urgency` (1.0).
- **Idle units and retirement (Q-SIM-84).** A Director unit with no job, order or path is idle; one idle for `director.idle_retire_s` (600 s, checked each window) is removed with no refund (a carrier still holding cargo is kept, so stock + cargo + consumed stays exact), reported as `UnitDespawned{reason: RETIRED}` (not a loss: no threat memory, stats or crew roll), so spent units never block `max_live_units`. `DirectorState.idle` (entity id as text -> the tick it was first seen idle) is in the snapshot and bounded on load.
- **STAGE and CAPTURE (Q-SIM-84).** STAGE is DONE when every current claimant has arrived and every slot is claimed or its expiry has passed, and FAILS with no claimant left (the chain fails with it). A CAPTURE whose claimants are still driving at its expiry gets a fresh expiry at most `tasking.capture_max_restarts` (3) times (`Job.restarts`, in the snapshot), then expires normally.
- **Escorts without raids.** With the cost at 10 and routine operations outside the ALPHA cap, ESCORT_CONVOYS also runs with no `convoy_attacks` (score 0.8); only the INTEL line needs a raid.
- **Load checks.** A snapshot is refused for a job waiting on itself or a later job, `dark` ticks past what the clock allows, a `blink_ready` at a node that does not exist (no upper bound: a hot reload may shorten `cooldown_s`, NX-401), a dark radar with no cooldown, and an idle unit that does not exist. The catalog loader refuses an effect operation with a cost, a job operation with cost 0 and duplicate preconditions or tiers. A hit on an already Disabled radar is not remembered. On strait-1 offensives fail at the strike step (jets lost to air defense or too few rounds); left to balance.
- **Tunable shapes (Q-DEV-21).** The formula shapes are fixed, every coefficient is a key: memory `1 + k c` / `1 / (1 + k c)` (`director.memory.factor`), weakness linear from `score.weakness_min` to `score.weakness_max`, Recon staleness `1 + f s` (`score.stale_factor`), radars per window (`decoy_blink.max_per_window`), the UP threshold (`adaptation_up_above`), ReinforceIADS' damage share (`reinforce_damage_above`), the fewest convoys kept (`escort_min_convoys`) and the front's reach (`frontline_hops`). Each default reproduces the behaviour before the key. The target value of a node the Static holds is `score.own_node_value`. Still in code: the noise draw's resolution (`NOISE_STEPS`, 1,000 steps: it sets the resolution of a draw and changing it reshuffles every seeded decision, so it is fixed with the RNG stream), and staleness 0 / 1 for a current / absent picture. `nexus/core` holds no other outcome-affecting literal (NX-377): a test fails on a new one, and the list of what stays (scales, performance constants, structure) is in CONFIG-AND-CONTENT §6.6.
- **Interpretations.** The Retake window is `director.retake_window_min` (30). Operations that target a coalition node need a `score.target_value` for its kind (no HQ or bridge). A STRIKE job posted on a node whose defenses are already down completes at once (a goal met). ESCORT_CONVOYS costs 10 (Q-SIM-82), so the Pattern of Life budget (10) can pay for it. The board holds at most `tasking.scout_open_max` (3) generated scout jobs per side, so the Static's moving units cannot flood the coalition's board with scout jobs (claimed ones stay, then those posted, then highest urgency, ties by key), posts at most one new one per `tasking.scout_post_interval_min` (2), and does not repost a cell for `tasking.scout_cooldown_min` after its job ended.

**Part 3 (NX-157): Pattern of Life (Q-SIM-85).** `nexus.core.launch` runs it after the window's operations; the pure parts (`patrol_via`, `convoy_base`, the garrison kind) are in `nexus.core.operations` and read only the view and `Own`.
- **Garrison upkeep.** `GARRISON_UPKEEP` (cost 8, priority 0.9, every tier, routine) has target `GARRISON`: the Static bases (not bridge or sanctuary) whose live `ENEMY_DIRECTOR` units with `Entity.home` = that base number fewer than the theater's `garrison_quota` (`Rules.garrison_quota`). Its one `STAGE` job buys one unit of the kind `director.garrison.kinds` gives (FOB: ENEMY_ARMOR; AIRBASE: ENEMY_FIGHTER, ENEMY_ARMOR; HQ: ENEMY_ARMOR, ENEMY_FIGHTER; the kind the base has fewest of, ties by list order), never reused from an idle unit and spawned at the base itself (up to `replace_per_window` units, each of the kind it is then shortest of, at the catalog cost each) (not by `plan_deploy`: a road trip through a neutral crossing would flip it), with `home` set. A live upkeep job on a base is not posted again. Retirement (`_retire_idle`) skips the lowest-id units up to the quota of their home base while the Static holds it (`_within_quota`); a unit with no home, or whose base fell, retires as before. `Own` carries each base's homed kinds and margin over the floor (negative below it).
- **Board.** `PATROL` is a job kind (`tasking.priority.patrol` 15, `fits`: any ground non-carrier). The board never assigns it, and gives a unit with a `home` only a DEFEND of that base (`_board_may_give`): the Director takes other jobs for them (`tasking.take`), and an operation never reuses one (`_reusable`). `Job.via` is the turning node; the claimant's path is the road to `via` then home (`_patrol_route`); DONE when a claimant is back at the base, FAILED with no claimant or when the base is lost.
- **Timers.** `last_patrol`, `last_convoy`, `last_offensive` (set when they fire; `last_offensive` only when an operation launched, so a failed attempt retries each window) and `last_flip`; due when `running tick - last >= period`, so they are frozen with the clock by a pause and independent of `sim.time_scale` (TD-1). They start at 0, so the first patrol is one period in.
- **Patrol.** Per Static base, the lowest-id idle ground garrison units homed there (`patrol_slots` per job, up to `patrols_per_base` jobs) take the job at posting; the base is skipped while its patrols are out or when no unit or turning node exists. `_recall` sends an idle homed unit away from its held home back (a road route for ground, straight for aircraft).
- **Convoy.** The `convoys_per_period` bases with the largest `missing x (GARRISON_UPKEEP cost) - (stock - floor)` (ties by id; above zero) each get a `RESUPPLY` job (key `op:POL_CONVOY:<base>:0`), taken by an idle carrier of `director.pol.convoy_kind` or a new one, within `max_spawns_per_window` and the tier's unit room; a new carrier costs `convoy_cost`, from the surplus. **In code (NX-413):** a base no truck can reach by the supply-run rule (`logistics.plan_run`: around nodes the coalition holds, over intact bridges) gets no convoy that period and nothing is spawned or paid; the period's slot is not passed to the next base.
- **Capture slot.** At Pattern of Life, when `offensive_period_s` has passed since the last launch and the cap is not spent, candidates are the catalog operations with a CAPTURE job whatever their tier, ranked as usual (one `director` draw each), selected against the real surplus (`director.surplus`) and the PoL unit room; the best one launches. The cap check (no live viewer unit and `night_capped`) also drops capture operations from the normal window at every tier.
- **Cap.** `capture` stamps `last_flip` on every Static flip, and holds a later bar at `CAPTURE_FULL - 1` while `night_capped` when there is no live viewer unit or the node is in `DirectorState.held`; the flip comes on the first tick after the cap ends (one node per cap: the others are held again). A patrolling unit is not a capturer.
- **Load checks.** A snapshot is refused for a timer past the clock or one no window could have set, a `home` that is not a node or is on a unit that is not the Director's, and a `via` on a job that is not a patrol. The loader refuses a garrison or convoy kind that is not an ENEMY mover that can stage or resupply, a node with a quota whose node kind has no garrison kinds, a quota with no GARRISON operation, and more than one GARRISON operation.
- **Interpretations.** (1) The patrol's turning node is the one toward the front, neutral or not (Q-SIM-86 replaces the held-only rule). (2) The counts per window or period are keys (Q-DEV-21), default 1; the convoy costs 0. (3) Probe-to-capture is not built: the slot takes the operations with a CAPTURE job (Offensive, Retake). (4) The PoL live-unit cap is 40 (24 in Q-SIM-78).

**Part 4 (NX-379): Recon that explores (Q-SIM-87).** RECON costs 2 (was 5) and its one job is `SCOUT at: sector_point` for an `ENEMY_RECON_UAV`. `nexus.core.operations.recon_point(view, rules, own, sector, kind)` resolves it, pure, from the `SideView` and `Own` (TD-2):
- **The cells.** The centres of a `director.recon.grid_m` grid (10,000 m, cells at `k x grid + grid / 2`) that lie in the sector. A cell's staleness is the fresher (lower) of two readings (TD-5, FOW-6a): the track reading is 1 with no track of any kind within `director.recon.loiter_radius_m` of its centre, else the age share (`age / fade`, at most 1; `fow.track_fade_s`) of the freshest track within it, 0 for a current one; the look reading is 1 for a cell no Static sensor covered within `director.recon.memory_s` (3,600 game s), else the time since over that time.
- **Visit memory.** `DirectorState.looked` (snapshot and hash) maps a cell's centre (`x:y`, m) to the running tick of the last decision window at which a Static unit's sensor covered it (every ENEMY unit with a sensor radius, emitting and not an off facility, id order, cells ascending; `launch.look`, run each window before the operations), whether or not any track was there. Marks older than `director.recon.memory_s` are dropped, so it holds only cells seen within that time (at most the theater's grid, 960 cells on strait-1); a restore refuses a key that is not the centre of a grid cell in the theater or a tick past the clock. Without it an empty cell stays 'unseen' however often a scout loiters there and the same cell is picked every window (6 distinct points in 6 game hours); with it the scouts spread over the sector.
- **The pick.** Cells in order of staleness, highest first, ties by (x, y). The first that passes: (1) the straight route from the base the scout is raised at (`launch_base`: the nearest Static base to the cell that spawns aircraft, is a landing base and has surplus over the forward-deploy margin, else the rear source; the same rule as `plan_deploy`) to the cell, widened by `loiter_radius_m`, crosses no marker with a ring whose last confirmed state is not Disabled (`tasking.ring_crossed`, the function `crosses_ring` applies to the board's markers, TB-2); (2) the scout can fly there, loiter and land at the nearest landing base with the bingo reserve left (`_sortie_fits`: fuel, speed and `loiter_s`; else it would turn for home mid-loiter). With no cell that passes, the sector is skipped this window. Only the outbound route is ring-checked: the return leg to the landing base after the loiter is not. Unknown rings are not avoided, as for every unit: a scout can still be shot down by a site the Static has not discovered.
- **The job.** The unit is raised at `launch_base`, which uses the same `logistics.deploy_pool` and `nearest_by_air` as `plan_deploy` so the two cannot drift. A point job never takes a second scout: the board's overfill (twice the slots when every job is full) does not apply to it. `Job.pos` is the point, `Job.node` and `Job.blob` are empty (the key stays `op:RECON:<sector>:0`); the unit is raised at `Where.origin`, the base the route was checked from, and a spare Director scout is reused only if it rests there. `Job.arrived` is the running tick the scout began to loiter (snapshot format 22; 21 is refused).
- **The loiter** (`tasking._loiter`). When a claimant has arrived (orbiting at the end of its path), `arrived` is set. Each board pass, a claimant with no path flies to the next corner of the square of half-diagonal `loiter_radius_m` around the point (E, S, W, N; the one after the corner nearest to it, clamped to the theater), so it stays within the radius. When `director.recon.loiter_s` has passed since `arrived` the job is DONE, the claimant goes home (`logistics.go_home`: RTB to the nearest landing base) and is a spare unit again once it lands. The job also ends DONE, and the scout goes home, when a ring discovered since the launch (not last seen Disabled) covers the point with the loiter radius of clearance. A restore refuses a job position outside the theater. A scout lost, or sent home at bingo, releases its claim; with no claimant `arrived` resets, and the job expires with its clock as any unclaimed job. A lost scout counts to `recon_losses` as before.
- **Known, not changed.** A 6 h QA run saw one 127.8 ms tick (p99 0.79 ms), not reproduced; tracked under NX-376.
- **Measured** (0 viewers, shipped config, 6 game hours, same seeds): see `services/sim/tests/golden/README.md` (snapshot format 22).

**Part 5 (NX-401): StrikeBridge, "cut the lifeline" (Q-SIM-112, Q-SIM-120, Q-SIM-121; lead decisions, owner may veto).** `STRIKE_BRIDGE` (cost 30, `director.op_priority.STRIKE_BRIDGE` 0.6, ALPHA and BRAVO, all PROPOSED) targets a NODE that is a bridge; its one job is `STRIKE at: target` for 2 `ENEMY_STRIKE_FIGHTER`s, with no STAGE (jets spawn at an airbase). The claimants fire on the bridge by the STRIKE claim rule (`bridges.target`, Q-SIM-124), and the job is DONE when the bridge is down (`tasking`).
- **Preconditions (closed list, `nexus.core.operations._holds`).** `target_is_bridge`; `target_bridge_intact` (not in the view's `bridge_down`, so a bridge under repair is never a target, Q-SIM-115); `target_feeds_coalition_node`; `target_bridge_off_cooldown`; `no_bridge_contested` (Q-SIM-121's "one bridge at a time", lead reading, owner may veto: no bridge is down and none is the node of a live STRIKE job on the side's board, `Own.struck`; and `select` takes at most one operation with this precondition per window, greedy in rank order, so BRAVO, with no `max_ops` cap and budget 80, never strikes both bridges in one window or in back-to-back windows; a yes/no rule, so no `max_down` key).
- **Feeds (`operations.fed_nodes`).** The nodes the other side holds whose kind has a `score.target_value` and whose supply route from that side's sanctuary (`Supply.rear`) crosses the bridge's edge. The route is `logistics.supply_path`, the rule its trucks drive by (Q-SIM-122, NX-407): intact roads (the view's `bridge_down`), entering no node the Static holds (neutral ground passable), the same helper `logistics.supply_route` calls with the world's owners, so the Director and the trucks agree on which bridge carries supply. A node only reachable through a Static base is fed by no bridge. Inputs are the roads, `SideView.owners` and `SideView.bridge_down` only: never tracks, units or occupancy (Q-SIM-112, Q-SIM-120), so units on or near the span change nothing.
- **Score.** The step-4 formula with the target-value x weakness factor taken as the largest `score.target_value.<kind>` x weakness (from that node's defense HP in the view) over the nodes the bridge feeds; nodes tied on it give the same factor, so the pick is order-free. Memory 1 (no counter); proximity (the bridge's sector) and noise as for any candidate.
- **Cooldown.** `DirectorState.bridge_ready` maps a bridge to the running tick from which it may be a target again: at `BridgeRepaired` (`bridges.repair` calls `provocation.bridge_repaired`), each directed side stamps the repair's running tick + `director.strike_bridge.cooldown_s` (1800 game s, PROPOSED) as ticks. A never-repaired bridge has no entry; entries are not pruned (at most one per bridge). In the snapshot (format 27) and the hash; a restore refuses a key that is not a bridge (no upper bound on the tick: a hot reload may have shortened the cooldown since the stamp).
